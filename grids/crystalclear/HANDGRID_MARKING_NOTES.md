**No colleague-shipped equivalence grid exists for this dataset.** Dataset 1 (`data/sample/dataset.csv`) shipped a hand-marked `topic_equivalence_grid.csv` that E8's `hand` rule was derived from and calibrated against (`.claude/scripts/data/E8_handgrids.json`). Crystalclear ships nothing of the kind. Every mark in `.claude/scripts/data/E9_handgrids_crystalclear.json` was made by E9-S4-P1 (this pass) from each cell's own `topic_definitions.json` (`Topic_Name` + `Definition_Text`) and, where the name alone was ambiguous, `topic_timeline.csv`'s `Topic` / `Chat Summary` columns, under the rules quoted verbatim below. **These are NOT colleague-authored marks** -- a reader must never mistake them for dataset-1-style precedent.

**Provenance (verbatim from the grids file):** No colleague-shipped equivalence grid exists for this dataset. Every mark here was made by E9-S4-P1 from each cell's own topic_definitions.json and topic_timeline.csv under the rules above. These are NOT colleague-authored marks.

## The two rules (verbatim from the grids file)

### `hand`

hand -- semantic equivalence. A discovered topic is Y for a manual label when the topic's name AND its Definition_Text cluster content plainly concern that label's subject. Sub-rules, binding: (1) exact near-duplicate label pairs are always marked identically under both rules: Sports (27) == Sprots (28), Email (7) == Mail (17), Mobile (18) == Phone (22) -- these are one label with two spellings supplied by the dataset. (2) The finance family is a containment hierarchy: Finance_Cryptocurreny (10) is a superset of Cryptocurrency (5) is a superset of Bitcoin (2), with Finance (9) = general finance. A crypto-named topic (name or definition about cryptocurrency, tokens, coins, wallets, exchanges, DeFi, staking, blockchain) is Y for ALL FOUR: Bitcoin, Cryptocurrency, Finance, Finance_Cryptocurreny. A generic finance topic (money, banking, payments-as-finance, investment, no crypto content) is Y for Finance and Finance_Cryptocurreny only. (3) Contentless-topic rule (amended 2026-09-10, dev-architect Amendment 2): a contentless (greeting-only or empty) discovered topic is N everywhere UNLESS a manual label names exactly that kind of content, in which case it is Y for that label alone -- concretely, a link-only topic is Y for Link only and N for every substantive label; a greeting-only or empty topic stays N everywhere because no greeting label exists among the 30.

### `strict`

strict -- literal name synonymy only. A discovered topic is Y for a manual label only when the topic's name is literally that label, after normalising case, underscores-to-spaces, and surrounding whitespace -- plus the three near-duplicate spelling-variant pairs (Sports/Sprots, Email/Mail, Mobile/Phone), which are always marked identically under both rules per sub-rule (1) above. Definition_Text is NOT consulted. No containment applies under strict: a topic literally named 'Cryptocurrency' is Y for Cryptocurrency only; a topic literally named 'Finance_Cryptocurreny' is Y for Finance_Cryptocurreny only -- the wider containment-aware reading is explicitly rejected (see open_questions) because strict's purpose is to be the mechanical lower bound hand is measured against, and a containment-aware strict would smuggle judgment back in.

## Near-duplicate and finance-family sub-rules (quoted, for emphasis)

- **Near-duplicate label pairs, always marked identically under both rules:** `Sports (27) == Sprots (28)`, `Email (7) == Mail (17)`, `Mobile (18) == Phone (22)`.
- **Finance family (containment under `hand` only, never under `strict`):** `Finance_Cryptocurreny (10)` is a superset of `Cryptocurrency (5)` is a superset of `Bitcoin (2)`, with `Finance (9)` = general finance. A crypto-named topic is `Y` for all four (Bitcoin, Cryptocurrency, Finance, Finance_Cryptocurreny); a generic-finance topic (no crypto content) is `Y` for Finance and Finance_Cryptocurreny only.
- **Contentless-topic rule (Amendment 2, dev-architect, 2026-09-10):** a contentless (greeting-only or empty) discovered topic is `N` everywhere unless a manual label names exactly that kind of content, in which case it is `Y` for that label alone -- concretely, a link-only topic is `Y` for `Link` only. No topic among the 112 marked here was found to be link-only or purely contentless; every discovered topic in this dataset carries substantive chat or spam text.
